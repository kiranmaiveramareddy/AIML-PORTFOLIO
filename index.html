A Java developer who got curious about AI

I started out as a full stack Java developer: Spring Boot services on the back end, React and TypeScript on the front, and everything in between that it takes to ship a feature and keep it running. Over six years that gave me a solid feel for building systems that stay up when traffic gets busy.

About a year ago I began working with AI in a serious way. I build GenAI agents in Python, write and test prompts, and connect them to the same Java services and APIs the business already depends on. I like the practical side of it: making an AI feature reliable, observable and safe enough to put in front of customers.

My experience sits in media, e-commerce and BFSI. Each one taught me something different about scale, speed and trust, and I bring all three to new projects.

I'm also a graduate student studying AI and machine learning. The projects further down are what came out of that coursework.

NLP and chatbot

Medical Chatbot: AI-powered health assistant

Goal
Build a conversational assistant that gives people clear, accessible health guidance using NLP.
How I built it
Designed intent recognition, curated a medical FAQ dataset, trained a sequence-to-sequence dialogue model, and improved it through user testing and accuracy checks.
Tools
Python, NLTK, TensorFlow / Keras, Flask, JSON intent dataset
Why it matters
It covers the full path from data prep to a deployed app, the same path I follow when adding an assistant to a business application.
Computer vision

Facial expression recognition using CNNs

Goal
Train a deep learning model that classifies facial expressions in real time.
How I built it
Preprocessed labeled images, designed a multi-layer CNN, used data augmentation, and judged results with confusion matrices and accuracy and loss curves.
Tools
Python, TensorFlow, Keras, OpenCV, NumPy, Matplotlib
Why it matters
It gave me hands-on practice designing, training and honestly evaluating a model, which applies to identity checks and fraud-style use cases in BFSI.
Generative AI

MindCheck: AI-powered mental health check-in assistant

Goal
Give college students a low-pressure, judgment-free way to check in on how they feel and get practical coping tips.
How I built it
Followed the Design Thinking process: researched student pain points, set clear ethical limits, designed the conversations, prototyped with a structured system prompt, and tested five scenarios including a crisis referral.
Tools
Chatbase.co, prompt engineering, Claude AI, Design Thinking
Why it matters
It shows I can build an AI assistant for a sensitive audience and set sensible boundaries around it.

Agentic AI and research
